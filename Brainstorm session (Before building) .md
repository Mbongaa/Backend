But for this use case — coffee and juice made at the kiosk on demand — Product Bundle may be more practical than BOM.

ERPNext describes Product Bundle as a “Sales Bill of Material,” where multiple stock items are sold together as one item. 

Example:

Orange Juice 350ml as a parent sales item:

Orange: 0.35 kg

Sugar: 0.01 kg

Cup: 1

The cashier sells “Orange Juice 350ml,” but the system deducts the component items from the kiosk warehouse.

For cakes, if they are prepared centrally and sent as finished units, we can treat cakes as finished stock. If they are assembled at the kiosk, use Product Bundle. If they are baked/produced in batches, use BOM/Work Order.

Verdict: Strong fit, but use the right structure:

Product Bundle for instant kiosk-made items.

BOM/Manufacturing for batch-produced cakes or prepared items.

Stock Entry for transfers, waste, and corrections.

Tax

The bigger Iraq issue is not tax. It is chart of accounts and statutory financial reporting format.

ERPNext has regional charts of accounts for many countries, but Iraq is not yet a standard localization in the official regional chart list. ERPNext’s docs describe country/regional chart-of-account templates, and a March 2026 ERPNext GitHub issue requests official support for the Iraqi Unified Accounting System.
